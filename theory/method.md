# Method — epistemic stance

How a slot-test is run, what its results count as, and the discipline that keeps the framework honest.

## Falsify, don't confirm

The real test is hunting a false positive — a work that fills every slot but feels nothing like the others. Finding one breaks the engine and teaches the most, cheaply. Two early hunts used *A Christmas Carol* (deliberately — a work that leaks into multiple engines is the strongest probe): it is a false positive for **unleashing** (refuses charity → ghost trigger → gives) and for **belonging** (solitary Scrooge → Fred's family → "Will you let me in, Fred?"), while really being redemption. Each hunt drove a slot revision (not a relabel of the work). Full records + verified quotes in [falsification-log.md](falsification-log.md).

## The recurring fix is a wish-valence guard

Both hunts produced the same shape of repair: structural slots are necessary but not sufficient — each engine needs a guard encoding that the protagonist *wants what the engine delivers*. Unleashing: the license must be *welcomed*, the catharsis *desired*. Belonging: the exclusion must be *involuntary*, the place *yearned-for*. *A Christmas Carol* fills three engines' bare slots because its plot (isolation → reconnection under supernatural pressure) contains those shapes; only redemption's slots encode a wish it services. The tags stay dumb about *theme* but must guard *wish-valence*.

The guard was stress-tested on a fresh non-Carol work and held: *The Call of the Wild* leaks under the old loose belonging slots (so the looseness was general, not a Carol artifact) but is correctly excluded by the revised ones — Buck's wish is dominance/the wild, not a tribe's acceptance ("he wanted... to have the leadership... He had earned it"; runs at the *head* of the pack). A hunt that fails to break a guarded engine strengthens it.

Each confirmed engine carries an explicit wish-valence guard:

- **repricing** — cross-currency worth
- **unleashing** — welcomed license
- **belonging** — yearned-for place
- **mastery** — capability earned through the regimen
- **redemption** — absolution earned through costly atonement

The mechanism the guard depends on — the interpreter's coherence is non-negotiable — is in [interpreter-and-engines.md](interpreter-and-engines.md).

## Cite everything, verbatim, to a primary source

Classifications produced from memory are suspect until checked. The P&P pass caught two misquotes that felt completely solid. The pre-commit gate (`cupel quotes-audit`) flags non-quoted prose in citation position; the rule is enforced by the linter, not just by discipline.

## Show contested readings, don't resolve them

Two citations offered for the same slot argue from the page. Composition is displayed — a work can fill more than one engine.

## The framework's own correctness is unvalidated until enough entries exist

Confirmed engines each have at least one clean specimen. Unleashing is falsification-tested both ways (Jekyll passes the revised slots, the Carol is excluded); the others' slot-fills are not the same as "the engine is sound." An open hypothesis from the two text-fed unleashing specimens: literary unleashing may come pre-moralized (wish granted then punished), where filmed unleashing (*John Wick*) serves it straight — connects to the text-fed-vs-embodied blind spot in [blind-spots.md](blind-spots.md).
