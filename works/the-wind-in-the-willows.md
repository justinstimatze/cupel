## The reading

**The bead.** The ache of having drifted from the small place that was yours, and the unbearable relief of being led back to it.

**Engines**
- **homecoming/reunion** · content · spine · ~ — The "Dulce Domum" chapter is the engine in its purest classic form. Mole is exiled from the burrow he abandoned the day he first found the river — "his old home that he had hurriedly forsaken and never sought again, that day when he first found the river" — and the summons reaches him as a literal call: "the rattle of the door-latch, the sudden firelight, and the sight of familiar things greeting us as long-absent travellers from far over-sea." The payout is the return itself: the Rat turning back ("We're going to find that home of yours, old fellow") until Mole's nose lands them at the little front door marked "Mole End." Held back by loss and exile; released by return to the prior place.

**The bundle.** Riverbank idyll, the terror and rescue of the Wild Wood, and Toad's farce all orbit the same warm center — but every road bends home.

**Dual-use read.** The counterfeit of homecoming is the regressive pull that refuses any growth — clinging so hard to the old burrow that the wider world becomes threat. The book does not run that; Mole keeps the river AND reclaims Mole End. It is the bright pole: return as enlargement, not retreat.

**Verdict.** A clean, confident homecoming engine — the canonical "going home" payout in English children's literature.
