## The reading

**The bead.** The Beatles' 1967 eighth studio album — released after the band's permanent retirement from touring — structured around the conceit that the album is performed by a fictional "Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band" that the Beatles invented, allowing them to *transform from the touring Beatles into someone else for an album-length performance*. The catalog's first specimen at music-album-as-form register, testing whether the engine framework applies cleanly to album-form.

**Engines (provisional — music-album-as-form test)**
- **the double life** · content · spine · ~ — at the *the Beatles performing as Sgt Pepper's band rather than as themselves* register. Slot-1 (the constraints of being-the-Beatles by 1966 — touring exhaustion; the Beatles-as-cultural-figures who can no longer perform without screaming-audience-overpowering-the-music; the structural-need to *do something different*); slot-2 (the album's elaborate sustained pretense that an entirely different fictional band is performing — the introduction-and-reprise framing as the structural mechanism); slot-3 (the savored-secret of the *fictional-band identity* permits the Beatles to *make music they could not make as the Beatles*). The album form supports the engine cleanly — the double-life is operationalized as the *fictional-band conceit*.

The album's substantive production-innovation (the *A Day in the Life* orchestral-crescendo; the four-track-bouncing technology; the genre-spanning composition across baroque-pop, music-hall, Indian-classical, psychedelia) is the engine's slot-2 work.
