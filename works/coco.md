## The reading

**The bead.** A boy crosses into the land of the dead and learns that the dead live exactly as long as the living remember them — and reunites a family split by a lie across the boundary of death.

**Engines**
- **legacy/transcendence** · content · spine · ~ — the purest statement of the engine: "Remember Me" — the dead persist only while remembered, and final death is being forgotten. The payout is remembrance as a form of survival, Héctor saved from oblivion by being restored to the family's memory.
- **homecoming/reunion** · content · also-runs · ~ — the family sundered by Héctor's exile reunited across the land of the dead; Miguel restoring the broken line.
- **belonging** · content · also-runs · ~ — the family (living and dead) made whole, the misfit musician boy folded back in.

**The bundle.** A legacy spine (remembrance as survival) carried by a cross-death reunion and the family restored — to be remembered *is* to belong.

**Dual-use read.** Legacy's counterfeit is the "your name will live forever" martyr-pitch; Coco is the enabling case — remembrance is earned through love and truth (the family's, not fame's: the celebrated De la Cruz is the fraud, the forgotten Héctor the real artist).

**Verdict.** A legacy/transcendence specimen at its most explicit — the dead live as long as they are remembered — with a family reunion as the payoff.
