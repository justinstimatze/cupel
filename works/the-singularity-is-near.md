## The evidence

### Apotheosis (spine)

Biological humanity's slowness as slot-1:

> "Although impressive in many respects, the brain suffers from severe limitations…the basic neural transactions are several million times slower than contemporary electronic circuits. That makes our physiological bandwidth for processing new information extremely limited compared to the exponential growth of the overall human knowledge base." (l. 585)

Slot-2 — accelerating-returns-driven merge:

> "Within several decades information-based technologies will encompass all human knowledge and proficiency, ultimately including the pattern-recognition powers, problem-solving skills, and emotional and moral intelligence of the human brain itself." (l. 583)

> "An increasingly intimate collaboration between our biological heritage and a future that transcends biology." (l. 534)

Slot-3 — the post-Singularity self:

> "Before the middle of this century, the growth rates of our technology—which will be indistinguishable from ourselves—will be so steep as to appear essentially vertical." (l. 591)

> "By the end of this century, the nonbiological portion of our intelligence will be trillions of trillions of times more powerful than unaided human intelligence." (l. 589)

Chapter 7's "Ich bin ein Singularitarian" (l. 342) and the section "The Singularity as Transcendence" (l. 350) name the leg explicitly.

### Mastery (also-runs)

The naive linear forecaster as slot-1:

> "Most long-range forecasts of what is technically feasible in future time periods dramatically underestimate the power of future developments because they are based on what I call the 'intuitive linear' view of history rather than the 'historical exponential' view." (l. 620)

Slot-2 — the framework + Kurzweil's longevity program:

> "I developed a theory I call the law of accelerating returns, which explains why technology and evolutionary processes in general progress in an exponential fashion." (l. 534)

> "I have been very aggressive about reprogramming my biochemistry. I take 250 supplements (pills) a day and receive a half-dozen intravenous therapies each week (basically nutritional supplements delivered directly into my bloodstream, thereby bypassing my GI tract)…Approaching this as an engineer, I measure dozens of levels of nutrients (such as vitamins, minerals, and fats), hormones, and metabolic by-products in my blood and other body samples." (l. 2879)

> "We consider the process of reversing and overcoming the dangerous progression of disease as a war. As in any war it is important to mobilize all the means of intelligence and weaponry that can be harnessed, throwing everything we have at the enemy." (l. 2881)

The reader operates with the framework as forecasting tool and the longevity program as bodily-engineering practice, prepared to merge with the post-biological self when the Singularity arrives.

### Order/legibility (also-runs)

The world misread as linear:

> "Exponential growth is deceptive. It starts out almost imperceptibly and then explodes with unexpected fury—unexpected, that is, if one does not take care to follow its trajectory." (l. 577)

The framework revealing it:

> "Exponential growth is a feature of any evolutionary process, of which technology is a primary example…The acceleration of progress and growth applies to each of them. Indeed, we often find not just simple exponential growth, but 'double' exponential growth, meaning that the rate of exponential growth (that is, the exponent) is itself growing exponentially." (l. 630)

> "We won't experience one hundred years of technological advance in the twenty-first century; we will witness on the order of twenty thousand years of progress (again, when measured by today's rate of progress), or about one thousand times greater than what was achieved in the twentieth century." (l. 620)

The chapter-2 graphs (Moore's Law extended back through five computing paradigms) supply the framework's empirical base.

### Impunity (also-runs)

The precautionary-constrained reader:

> "The Precautionary Principle. As Bostrom, Freitas, and other observers including myself have pointed out, we cannot rely on trial-and-error approaches to deal with existential risks. There are competing interpretations of what has become known as the 'precautionary principle.' (If the consequences of an action are unknown but judged by some scientists to have even a small risk of being profoundly negative, it's better to not carry out the action than risk negative consequences.)" (l. 5592)

The proactionary principle as the structural move against it:

> "Relinquishment, however, is not the appropriate response and will only interfere with the profound benefits of these emerging technologies while actually increasing the likelihood of a disastrous outcome. Max More articulates the limitations of the precautionary principle and advocates replacing it with what he calls the 'proactionary principle,' which involves balancing the risks of action and inaction." (l. 5592)

> "Broad relinquishment would require a totalitarian system to implement, and a totalitarian brave new world is unlikely because of the democratizing impact of increasingly powerful decentralized electronic and photonic communication." (l. 5622)

> "Suffering in the world continues and demands our steadfast attention. Should we tell the millions of people afflicted with cancer and other devastating conditions that we are canceling the development of all bioengineered treatments because there is a risk that these same technologies may someday be used for malevolent purposes? Having posed this rhetorical question, I realize that there is a movement to do exactly that, but most people would agree that such broad-based relinquishment is not the answer." (l. 5657)

Relinquishment is reframed as itself the catastrophe-risk, voiding the precautionary calculus. The cluster's e/acc-pole impunity-leg in its 2005 founding form. Andreessen 2023's *Techno-Optimist Manifesto* ([The Techno-Optimist Manifesto](/cupel/works/the-techno-optimist-manifesto/)) is the direct lineal descendant.

### Legacy/transcendence (also-runs)

Distinct from apotheosis (which is transcendence of biology into the merge); legacy here is indefinite life-extension itself:

> "Nanobots will have myriad roles within the human body, including reversing human aging (to the extent that this task will not already have been completed through biotechnology, such as genetic engineering)." (l. 808)

> "The Singularity will allow us to transcend these limitations of our biological bodies and brains. We will gain power over our fates. Our mortality will be in our own hands. We will be able to live as long as we want (a subtly different statement from saying we will live forever)." (l. 589)

The parenthetical is the text's only acknowledgment of slot-2-cost — a definitional dodge, not engagement with the resource-allocation, social-structure, and ecological-footprint costs of universal indefinite life-extension. Standard cluster-pure-counterfeit signature.
