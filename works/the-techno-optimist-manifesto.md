## The evidence

### Apotheosis (spine)

The reader as inheritor of a "deranged age":

> "We are told to denounce our birthright – our intelligence, our control over nature, our ability to build a better world." (Lies, l. 98-99)

The path to ascension — techno-capital machine + intelligence takeoff:

> "We believe we are poised for an intelligence takeoff that will expand our capabilities to unimagined heights." (Intelligence, l. 369-370)

> "We believe Artificial Intelligence is our alchemy, our Philosopher's Stone – we are literally making sand think." (Intelligence, l. 372-373)

> "We believe in deliberately and systematically transforming ourselves into the kind of people who can advance technology." (Becoming Technological Supermen, l. 540-541)

The ascended state — Technological Supermen at species scale:

> "We believe the global population can quite easily expand to 50 billion people or more, and then far beyond that as we ultimately settle other planets." (Abundance, l. 505-507)

> "We believe this is why our descendents will live in the stars." (Technology, l. 172)

> "We believe the ultimate mission of technology is to advance life both on Earth and in the stars." (Abundance, l. 512-513)

> "We are not victims, we are conquerors." (Becoming Technological Supermen, l. 573-574)

The elevation is transcendence of all limits — not Yudkowsky+Soares's surviving-humanity-self but the full post-human / cosmic-empire vision.

### Order/legibility (also-runs)

The reader inside the "demoralization campaign":

> "Our present society has been subjected to a mass demoralization campaign for six decades – against technology and against life." (The Enemy, l. 701-702)

> "We are being lied to." (Lies, l. 84)

The framework revealing it:

> "Combine technology and markets and you get what Nick Land has termed the techno-capital machine, the engine of perpetual material creation, growth, and abundance." (The Techno-Capital Machine, l. 315-317)

> "We believe the techno-capital machine of markets and innovation never ends, but instead spirals continuously upward." (The Techno-Capital Machine, l. 319-320)

> "We believe in accelerationism – the conscious and deliberate propulsion of technological development – to ensure the fulfillment of the Law of Accelerating Returns. To ensure the techno-capital upward spiral continues forever." (The Techno-Capital Machine, l. 343-346)

The closed loop:

> "We believe everything good is downstream of growth." (Technology, l. 136)

> "We believe there is no material problem – whether created by nature or by technology – that cannot be solved with more technology." (Technology, l. 174-175)

> "Give us a real world problem, and we can invent technology that will solve it." (Technology, l. 192-193)

Every objection is reframed as failure-to-grok the framework; every counterexample as evidence-against-the-enemies.

### Impunity (also-runs)

The Precautionary Principle as the constraint:

> "Our present society has been subjected to a mass demoralization campaign for six decades – against technology and against life – under varying names like 'existential risk', 'sustainability', 'ESG', 'Sustainable Development Goals', 'social responsibility', 'stakeholder capitalism', 'Precautionary Principle', 'trust and safety', 'tech ethics', 'risk management', 'de-growth', 'the limits of growth'." (The Enemy, l. 701-706)

The exemption — jettisoning it "with extreme prejudice":

> "Our enemy is the Precautionary Principle, which would have prevented virtually all progress since man first harnessed fire. The Precautionary Principle was invented to prevent the large-scale deployment of civilian nuclear power, perhaps the most catastrophic mistake in Western society in my lifetime. The Precautionary Principle continues to inflict enormous unnecessary suffering on our world today. It is deeply immoral, and we must jettison it with extreme prejudice." (The Enemy, l. 743-749)

The load-bearing impunity statement:

> "We believe any deceleration of AI will cost lives. Deaths that were preventable by the AI that was prevented from existing is a form of murder." (Intelligence, l. 384-386)

> "Our enemy is the ivory tower, the know-it-all credentialed expert worldview, indulging in abstract theories, luxury beliefs, social engineering, disconnected from the real world, delusional, unelected, and unaccountable – playing God with everyone else's lives, with total insulation from the consequences." (The Enemy, l. 731-735)

Defection from precautionary ethics reframed as moral imperative; the cost of constraint reframed as murder. The catalog's most-extreme load-bearing impunity-leg specimen.

The Marinetti import:

> "To paraphrase a manifesto of a different time and place: 'Beauty exists only in struggle. There is no masterpiece that has not an aggressive character. Technology must be a violent assault on the forces of the unknown, to force them to bow before man.'" (Becoming Technological Supermen, l. 565-568)

The "different time and place" elides Marinetti's 1909 Futurist Manifesto, which is widely cited in the scholarship as an aesthetic precursor to Italian Fascism (the elevation of struggle, violence, and force as virtue). The quotation is imported without that attribution; the elision is the structural move worth flagging.

### Mastery (also-runs)

The reader as not-yet a Technological Superman:

> "We believe in deliberately and systematically transforming ourselves into the kind of people who can advance technology. We believe this certainly means technical education, but it also means going hands on, gaining practical skills, working within and leading teams – aspiring to build something greater than oneself." (Becoming Technological Supermen, l. 540-547)

The trainable craft:

> "We believe in radical competence." (Technological Values, l. 617)

> "We believe in ambition, aggression, persistence, relentlessness – strength." (Technological Values, l. 588-589)

> "We believe in the Silicon Valley code of 'pay it forward', trust via aligned incentives, generosity of spirit to help one another learn and grow." (Technological Values, l. 651-653)

The Hero's Journey-as-builder:

> "We believe in adventure. Undertaking the Hero's Journey, rebelling against the status quo, mapping uncharted territory, conquering dragons, and bringing home the spoils for our community." (Becoming Technological Supermen, l. 561-563)

> "We believe in greatness. We admire the great technologists and industrialists who came before us, and we aspire to make them proud of us today." (Becoming Technological Supermen, l. 580-582)

Competence-as-virtue-bundle at the radical-competence / Silicon-Valley / Hero's-Journey sub-mode — distinct from Yudkowsky+Soares's import-by-reference register and from MacAskill's EA career-capital register.
