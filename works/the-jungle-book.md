## The evidence

Belonging holds the protagonist back by having no place, and releases them by recognition-as-kin: a named tribe takes them in, and they are content to let the world stay wrong. This is the engine's first slot-test against a page — belonging was the one confirmed engine never checked. "Mowgli's Brothers," the opening story, is near-diagrammatic for the slots.

### Slot 1 — Exclusion (cast out / with no place)

Mowgli enters the jungle as a placeless human infant — no tribe, no claim on anyone:

> "Directly in front of him, holding on by a low branch, stood a naked brown baby who could just walk" (ll. 194–195)

and his non-belonging is stated aloud, by the predator who claims him and in the very name the wolves use for themselves — the question of whether a man's cub has any place among them:

> "The cub is mine. Give him to me. What have the Free People to do with a man's cub?" (Shere Khan, ll. 318–319)

The placeless outsider, claimed by a hunter, with the tribe itself asking what it has to do with him: the exclusion is on the record.

### Slot 2 — Group (a specific named tribe)

The tribe is named twice over — by its formal name and by its name for itself:

> "Mowgli was entered into the Seeonee Wolf Pack" (l. 405)

> "What have the Free People to do with the orders of any save the Free People?" (Akela, ll. 319–321)

The Seeonee Wolf Pack, who call themselves the Free People: a specific, named tribe, not a diffuse "society."

### Slot 3 — Acceptance (taken in by that group)

The acceptance is a literal scene — the Council Rock, where by the Law a cub must be spoken for. Baloo speaks:

> "Let him run with the Pack, and be entered with the others. I myself will teach him." (ll. 342–343)

the Pack assents and Bagheera pays the price:

> "Let him run with the Pack. Where is the bull, Bagheera? Let him be accepted." (ll. 377–378)

and the taking-in is sealed:

> "And that is how Mowgli was entered into the Seeonee Wolf Pack for the price of a bull and on Baloo's good word." (ll. 405–406)

The recognition is specifically **as kin** — Mother Wolf names him, the act that makes him family:

> "O thou Mowgli--for Mowgli the Frog I will call thee" (l. 277)

### Result

All three slots fill cleanly with verified quotes, against a literal adoption ceremony. Tagged **belonging**, and it is the engine's first validation against a page — which completes the confirmed-three: repricing ([*P&P*](/cupel/works/pride-and-prejudice/)), unleashing (*Jekyll/Hyde*), belonging (*The Jungle Book*) now each have a clean specimen.

Belonging's distinguishing mark holds here: the prize is a *tribe's* acceptance, not the world's recognition. Mowgli wants the Pack, not the human village; the recognition is kinship ("for Mowgli the Frog I will call thee," "on Baloo's good word"), and he is content to let the world of men stay wrong about him. That is what separates belonging from repricing — there is no market mispricing his worth, only a tribe deciding whether he is kin.
