## The reading

**The bead.** That an unwanted, placeless orphan — sent by mistake, nearly sent back — is taken in *as she is* and given a permanent home, becoming so wholly of the place that it renames her.

**Engines**
- **belonging** · content · spine · ~ — the orphan with no place, given one that holds. Anne arrives as the wrong child (a girl where a boy was ordered) and is almost returned — "We sent word, Matthew and I, for you to bring us a boy from the asylum… we wanted a boy ten or eleven years old" — but the placeless child is taken in anyway: Matthew's "We might be some good to her" turns the wrong-arrival into a home, and she ends the book belonging so fully that the house is in her name. The textbook belonging shape (cf. Mowgli, [The Jungle Book ("Mowgli's Brothers")](/cupel/works/the-jungle-book/)): involuntarily placeless → taken into a company that holds → a place that is *hers*.

**The bundle.** Single dominant wish (the found home), with a being-desired adjacency that doesn't separate cleanly here — Anne's longing to be *wanted* reads as the same wish to be given a place, not a distinct relational desire. Spine is belonging.

**Dual-use read.** Belonging's counterfeit is the cult / the cell — a place bought by self-erasure and submission ([counterfeit-catalog](/cupel/theory/counterfeit-catalog/), *Fight Club*). Anne is the bright pole, un-run: the place is freely given and she is kept *as she is* (Marilla takes her despite the chatter and the temper she never sheds). No counterfeit is sold; the engine runs in its genuine form.

**Verdict — belonging, clean.** A high-recognition childhood classic that fills belonging's slots on a famous, citable scene. Tagged `~ reviewed` from a reading with the key scene confirmed against the text; a verbatim `entries/` dossier would promote it to `✓`.
