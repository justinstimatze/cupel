## The reading

**The bead.** Death is not an ending: every moment you ever lived is still happening somewhere in time, so the people you lost — and you yourself — are permanently, indestructibly alive.

**Engines**
- **legacy/transcendence** · content · spine · ~ — the machine is the defeat of mortality. Billy Pilgrim comes "unstuck in time" and learns the Tralfamadorian frame in which all moments coexist forever and no one is ever truly dead, only dead "in this particular moment" — the catastrophe of Dresden, the catastrophe of one's own death, dissolved into a structure where nothing the self contains can perish. "So it goes" is not resignation but the reader's release from finitude. In-copyright, so tagged from the book's widely-known structure, no quote.

**The bundle.** Single-engine: a legacy/transcendence spine delivered as cosmic anesthesia rather than glory — the name that outlives death recast as the moment that never stops existing.

**Dual-use read.** Legacy/transcendence's counterfeit is the comforting lie that lets you stop grieving and stop resisting — fatalism dressed as enlightenment. Slaughterhouse-Five is the rare case that runs the engine and interrogates its own counterfeit: the Tralfamadorian "all time exists" both consoles the trauma survivor and excuses the inaction that lets the bombs fall. It sells transcendence over mortality while quietly asking whether that transcendence is wisdom or shell-shock.

**Verdict.** The catalog's mortality-defeating engine in its bleakest key — eternity offered as balm to a man who cannot bear to have death be real.
