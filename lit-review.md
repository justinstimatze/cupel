# Prior art & literature review

*This document answers the standard prior-art objection — that the engine taxonomy reinvents an existing story-shape wheel. Short version: the **engines** overlap existing scholarship a lot; the **axis** cupel sorts on, and the **dual-use thesis**, are where it's genuinely new. Citations were web-verified during the original literature survey; flagged items need a primary-source read before quoting.*

## The core finding

Almost every prior framework sorts stories on one of two axes, **neither of which is cupel's**:

- **Plot structure / event-sequence / role-grammar** — Aristotle (*mythos*, *anagnorisis*), Propp (31 functions), Campbell + Vogler (the monomyth), Polti (36 dramatic situations), Tobias (20 master plots), Snyder (*Save the Cat!* genres).
- **Emotional trajectory (valence over time)** — Vonnegut's "shapes of stories"; Reagan et al. 2016, "The emotional arcs of stories are dominated by six basic shapes" (*EPJ Data Science*; the six: Rags-to-Riches rise, Tragedy fall, Man-in-a-Hole fall-rise, Icarus rise-fall, Cinderella rise-fall-rise, Oedipus fall-rise-fall).

cupel sorts on a **third axis: which wish the work gratifies** (a named stuck-state + the release the reader paid for). No surveyed framework is *organized by* desire-being-satisfied. The proof that this is a real, different dimension: **"belonging" has no home as a primary category in Booker, Tobias, or Snyder** — a found-family/taken-in wish appears only as a sub-beat — and conversely one plot-shape (Booker's Rags-to-Riches) splits across several cupel engines (abundance / repricing / being-desired) depending on which payoff fires. Plot-shape and engine are independent.

## Nearest prior art, by closeness

**Closest of all — John Cawelti, *Adventure, Mystery, and Romance* (Univ. Chicago Press, 1976).** Enumerates popular **formulas** (adventure, romance, mystery, melodrama, alien-beings/fantasy) and assigns each a distinct **"moral fantasy"** — the reader-wish it resolves. This is structurally cupel's move ("each engine = a wish + its payoff"). **cupel's delta:** Cawelti's unit stays *genre-shaped* (formula = recognizable genre); cupel **decouples the engine from genre**, so repricing can run inside SF, romance, or literary fiction. Cite Cawelti as the direct ancestor or a reviewer will say cupel reinvented him.

**A. J. Greimas, the actantial model (*Sémantique structurale*, 1966).** The structuralist scheme closest of all to cupel's axis: narrative organized by six *actants* on three axes — **desire** (subject → object), communication (sender → receiver), and power (helper / opponent). The subject→object pair is explicitly the *axis of desire*, i.e. a sort-by-wish. **cupel's delta:** Greimas formalizes the grammar of *one character's desire within the tale*; cupel sorts by the *reader's* gratified wish across tales and adds the dual-use counterfeit. Greimas is the strongest "you reinvented structuralism" objection — cite him, and locate the novelty in the *axis + dual-use*, not the slot-decomposition (which simply *is* structuralism's method, Propp → Greimas → Barthes → Todorov). *(Verify the actant terms against the primary text before quoting; the desire/communication/power axis labels are standard but confirm.)*

**The writing-craft desire canon — McKee, *Story* (1997); Egri, *The Art of Dramatic Writing* (1946); Stanislavski, the "objective"/"super-objective" (*An Actor Prepares*, 1936).** The most-taught form of "story = desire": a protagonist pursues an object of desire against antagonism; "want vs need" is its standard refinement. This is the nearest *practitioner* prior art and the objection an MFA reader raises first. **cupel's delta (load-bearing):** the craft canon analyzes the **protagonist's want** as a tool for *building* plot and conflict; cupel names the **reader's wish** the finished work *gratifies* — a different object (the reader enjoys John Wick's catharsis though Wick is *dragged* in, not wanting it). Conflating the two is the single easiest way to dismiss cupel as relabeled McKee; the README now marks the seam in its lead. *(Verify McKee/Egri/Stanislavski attributions and any wording against primary sources before quoting — these were added from general knowledge, not a fresh source check.)*

**Northrop Frye, *Anatomy of Criticism* (1957).** The four mythoi (Comedy/spring, Romance/summer, Tragedy/autumn, Irony/winter). His **Romance = the wish-fulfillment mode** is the one place prior theory names a wish-axis — but it's one undifferentiated bucket. **cupel effectively unbundles Fryeʼs single "Romance" into several engines.**

**Christopher Booker, *The Seven Basic Plots* (2004).** Overcoming the Monster, **Rags to Riches**, Quest, Voyage and Return, Comedy, Tragedy, **Rebirth**. Uniquely asks *why we tell stories* (Jungian function). Richest term-overlap: Rags-to-Riches ≈ abundance/repricing; Rebirth ≈ redemption/transformation. **cupel's delta:** Booker's units are still named as plot-shapes with psychology attached as interpretation; cupel makes the wish the classifier.

**Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan; Ryan & Deci 2000, *American Psychologist*).** Three basic needs: **competence, relatedness, autonomy**. **mastery ≈ competence and belonging ≈ relatedness are essentially SDT needs renamed** — cite explicitly to preempt the "you reinvented Deci & Ryan" objection. Maslow's esteem level ≈ repricing. **Caution (mapping to fix):** autonomy ↛ unleashing. SDT autonomy = volitional self-endorsement; unleashing = licensed *magnitude* of force. Near-miss, not identity; unleashing maps better to the "power fantasy" vocabulary than to SDT.

**Salmon & Symons, *Warrior Lovers* (2001).** The work cupel's **gendered-bundle** observation most reinvents: female erotic fantasy (romance) = securing a devoted, high-investment mate, heroine experienced as uniquely irresistible — ≈ cupel's romance bundle (being-desired + abundance + protective partner). **cupel's delta:** (a) decomposes the monolithic "romance fantasy" into finer engines; (b) frames the masculine bundle as competence/power (unleashing + mastery), where Symons's male pole is *porn* (physical access), not the action fantasy. Adopt their *descriptive* split without committing to the contested evo-psych causal story. Corroborating reader-response evidence: **Radway, *Reading the Romance* (1984)** (compensatory nurturance/being-cared-for) and **Modleski, *Loving with a Vengeance* (1982)**; pop-culture articulation: **Dowling, *The Cinderella Complex* (1981)**.

**Freud, "Creative Writers and Day-Dreaming" (1908)** is cupel's deepest root (fiction = disguised wish-fulfillment) but supplies only one mechanism and two coarse wish-types (ambitious / erotic) — the premise, not the taxonomy. **Jung** (archetypes, shadow, individuation) supplies naming substrate for unleashing (shadow-integration) and transformation, but explicitly rejects the wish-fulfillment frame. **Vernacular**: TV Tropes already names *Power Fantasy* (≈ unleashing / apotheosis) and *Competence Porn* (≈ mastery) — cupel consolidates folk terms, it didn't coin the concept.

## cupel's two claims beyond cataloguing wishes

**Claim 1 — the dual-use thesis (the same wish-engine powers benign fiction *and* its counterfeit).** The two halves are well-developed separately: **Kruglanski's 3N model** (Need–Narrative–Network significance-quest → radicalization) and **Hoffer's *True Believer* (1951)** (the frustrated drawn by belonging/revenge/recognition — strikingly close to a *catalog of recruiting wishes*) on the recruitment side; **Green & Brock's transportation theory (2000)** proving story-immersion shifts attitudes (the bridge mechanism); and **Ellul's *Propaganda* (1962)** (exploit the existing need, supply the pseudo-satisfaction). **Nearest prior art: Linda Schlegel (2021), "Storytelling against extremism"** — explicitly says extremists exploit fiction's transportation engine and proposes turning the *same engine* to counter-messaging (a real same-machine-two-uses move). **What's novel in cupel:** the *systematization* — a **typed catalog of engines, each paired one-to-one with a distinct counterfeit** (belonging→cult, unleashing→grievance-radicalization, abundance→MLM). No source builds that one-to-one dual-use taxonomy; they assert it for a single engine or domain. *Verdict: substantially novel as a unifying framework; not novel as an isolated observation.*

**Claim 2 — the method (a Bechdel-style, verbatim-quote-gated, falsification-tested classifier for the wish a story serves).** The **form** (Bechdel–Wallace test, 1985, + descendants: Mako Mori, sexy-lamp, Vito Russo) is acknowledged prior art — but every Bechdel descendant tests **representation**, not desire. The **verbatim-evidence-grounding** primitive also exists (DesireDB / "desire fulfillment in text"; evidence-grounded narrative classification in NLP fact-checking) — but as ML annotation pipelines, not as a citation-gated, false-positive-hunting *test*. **Not found anywhere: the combination applied to cupel's target.** *Verdict: the operationalization is novel; the borrowed primitives are not.*

## Bottom line (how to position cupel honestly)

- **Soft-pedal as novel:** "fiction gratifies wishes" (Freud); mastery≈competence, belonging≈relatedness (SDT); the bare gendered split (Symons). Frame cupel as *synthesizing and operationalizing* these.
- **Genuine deltas:** (1) re-axis to the **reader's gratified wish** — *not* the protagonist's dramatic objective (the delta vs the McKee/Egri craft canon) and not plot-shape/trajectory, holding structure constant; (2) **decouple engine from genre** (cupel's edge over Cawelti and Greimas); (3) a **single closed taxonomy** unifying the literary tradition (Cawelti), motivation psychology (SDT), and fan vernacular (power fantasy / competence porn); (4) the **one-to-one dual-use taxonomy**; (5) the **Bechdel-style citation-gated falsification-tested test** for wishes.
- **Engine names with near-synonyms in prior art** (so claim the *axis*, not the labels): repricing≈Rags-to-Riches/esteem; abundance≈Rags-to-Riches; redemption≈Rebirth; transformation≈Metamorphosis (Tobias); mastery≈competence/competence-porn; belonging≈relatedness; being-desired≈the romance "moral fantasy"; election≈the hero archetype.

## Caveats flagged by the survey

- Schlegel (2021) PDF is image-encoded — thesis verified from the abstract page; read the full article before treating it as the load-bearing dual-use precedent.
- Cawelti's per-formula "moral fantasy" wording and Salmon & Symons's feature-lists were reconstructed from secondary summaries; confirm against the books before quoting verbatim (the Symons "always/never adaptive" line *is* verified).
- "Competence porn" attributed to John Rogers (~2009): plausible, widely repeated, not confirmed to a primary source.
- BITE model and Terror Management Theory both carry validity/replication disputes; cite as contested.
- A targeted search of media-psychology / "dark persuasion" trade books was not done — that's where a competing dual-use framing would most likely hide.
- **No inter-rater-reliability, null-model, or base-rate study has been run.** The slot-test's claim to be "more disciplined than taste" is argued, not measured; a hostile methodologist's first asks — does an independent reader fill the same slots? what fraction of random novels can fill *any* given engine? — are unanswered.
- **Citation audit (2026-05-27, "no hallucination" pass).** All **61 Gutenberg IDs across the project** were verified against gutenberg.org Title/Author headers — every one resolves to the cited work. The collection/anthology cases are all legitimate (the cited tale or poem lives inside): Aladdin → #128 *Arabian Nights Entertainments*; Ali Baba → #20916 *Arabian Nights: Best-known Tales*; Three Little Pigs → #7439 *English Fairy Tales*; Red-Headed League → #1661 *Adventures of Sherlock Holmes*; Gold-Bug → #2147 *Works of Poe v.1*; Invictus → #1568 Henley's *Poems*; Raffles → #706 *The Amateur Cracksman*. `#10253` (Carnegie) appears **only** as a cautionary-404 example, never as a live citation. The scholarly specifics most prone to drift were confirmed too: **arXiv:1606.07772** (Reagan et al.) and **Ryan & Deci, *American Psychologist* 55 (2000), 68–78**, plus the four prior-art works added this session — **Greimas, *Sémantique structurale* (1966)**; **McKee, *Story* (1997)**; **Egri, *The Art of Dramatic Writing* (1946)**; **Stanislavski, *An Actor Prepares* (1936)** — titles, dates, and the actantial-axis labels all check out. (Confirm any *verbatim* wording from those four before quoting — they are cited as works/concepts here, not quoted.) The remaining well-known scholarly books (Cawelti, Frye, Booker, Green & Brock, Kruglanski, etc.) rest on the original three-thread survey; **Schlegel 2021** is still the one flagged for a full-text read before it carries load.
